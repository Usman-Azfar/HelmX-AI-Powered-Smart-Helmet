INTERPRETATION (per the source doc's own Scenario framework)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This matches SCENARIO 1 exactly: A1 = no reconstruction triggers, B =
reconstruction triggers, A2 = no reconstruction triggers. Per the doc's own
rule: "Strong evidence that the acquisition-path change is associated with
the altered reconstruction behavior under this matched test." A1 and A2
being not just similar but IDENTICAL in this metric (both exactly zero,
zero variance) is about as clean a return-to-baseline as this kind of test
can produce -- rules out time drift, gradual environmental change, or
gradual sensor/temperature change as an explanation (Scenario 3, "A1 differs
from A2 substantially," is clearly NOT what happened -- A1 and A2 are
identical on every reconstruction-related metric).

Per the doc's explicit instruction, the PRECISE MECHANISM is NOT claimed here.
One coherent, plausible hypothesis worth stating (not proven, not the only
possibility): the polling path takes one on-demand I2C transaction per
20ms tick; if the sensor is genuinely undisturbed (this desk, no vibration),
consecutive raw digitized ADC readings can be bit-for-bit identical at this
read cadence (i.e. the physical/electrical noise floor at 50Hz single-shot
sampling can sit below one LSB of change), which would make every window
in the buffer literally constant -- and a model whose reconstruction task is
learned on real (non-constant) motion could plausibly reconstruct a truly
constant window to near-perfect (here, INT8-quantized to bit-exact zero)
error. The FIFO path samples the SAME physically-static scene at a much
higher native rate (~480-495Hz measured) before software-decimating 1-in-10
-- if the sensor's real analog/ADC noise floor is not exactly stationary
at that faster timescale, the decimated ~50Hz samples it hands to the model
would carry slightly more genuine sample-to-sample variation than the
polling path's samples do, even under identical macroscopic stillness --
enough to move the window off the "perfectly constant" input the model
reconstructs to zero error, without moving it anywhere near "crash-like."
This is offered as the most parsimonious explanation consistent with every
number above, NOT as a proven mechanism -- confirming it would need directly
comparing raw sample bit-patterns between the two paths (out of scope here).

CLAIM LIMITS (per the doc's own "IMPORTANT CLAIM LIMITS" section)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This result does NOT establish, and should NOT be cited as establishing:
- that FIFO acquisition is inherently unsafe or inferior,
- that polling acquisition is inherently superior (the "zero error" result
  it produces here may equally reflect an idle test condition that doesn't
  resemble real riding, where the sensor is never truly motionless),
- a specific internal mechanism for why the two paths diverge,
- that this generalizes to all MPU6050/MPU6500 systems or mountings,
- any real-road false-alert-rate conclusion (both conditions were stationary,
  desk-based, unattended captures -- see Section V's existing claim limits).

What IS established: under carefully matched, back-to-back, same-session
conditions with everything else held constant, switching only the
acquisition method from polling to FIFO/decimated reproducibly and
completely changes the reconstruction-error distribution from "always
exactly zero" to "always measurably nonzero, occasionally above threshold."
This resolves the open question left by the E2/E3/E4/H2 round
(acquisition_compare/acquisition_comparison.txt) in favor of the acquisition
method being the driving factor, not coincidence -- the E2/E3 finding was
real and reproducible, not a fluke.

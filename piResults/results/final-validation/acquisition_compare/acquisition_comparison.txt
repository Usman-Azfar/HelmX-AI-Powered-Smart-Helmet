FINDING -- reconstruction-only marginal triggers, not seen in the E1 baseline
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All 5 confirmed triggers in the E2 run had crash_prob=0.0 (classifier silent)
and recon_error in [10.99, 13.35] -- just above the deployed recon_error
threshold of 10.8787 (1-23% over). The E1-reused baseline (1975 windows) had
zero triggers, and the earlier guided benign-motion session (I, 11 phases,
2026-09-19/20) found the reconstruction branch had ZERO threshold crossings
across all of them.

Both runs were nominally idle/stationary (unattended captures, nothing
deliberately done to the sensor). Two honest possibilities, NEITHER confirmed
here:
  1. The FIFO/decimation acquisition path introduces different sample timing
     or aliasing versus the deployed polling read, marginally raising
     reconstruction error during otherwise-idle periods.
  2. Coincidental environmental disturbance (e.g. desk vibration, thermal
     drift -- temperature rose from ~43C to a max of 53C over the 20-min E2
     run per resources.temperature_c) during the E2 run specifically, unrelated
     to the acquisition method.
This is reported as an open, disclosed finding for the paper's E1/E2
acquisition-method discussion -- NOT claimed as a FIFO-caused effect, since
that would require an isolating experiment (e.g. rerunning E1's standalone
polling baseline for the same 20-min duration under identical conditions)
that hasn't been done. All 5 crossings are marginal (recon_error within ~2.5
of the 10.88 threshold, versus e.g. the K-section's known outlier reconstruction
error of ~751 on an actual crash window) -- not remotely comparable in
magnitude to a real crash signature.

E3 (SCHED_FIFO + CPU-pinned, same acquisition method as E2) -- confirms and sharpens
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The E3 run (source: acquisition_compare/e3_sched_enhanced.json, sudo chrt -f 10
taskset -c 3, cpu_affinity confirmed [3]) is otherwise identical in method to E2
(same FIFO/decimation acquisition, same 20-min duration, same idle/stationary
condition) -- and independently produced the SAME finding: 5 confirmed triggers
over 3962 windows, again all reconstruction-only (crash_prob=0.0 every time).

More striking: the 5 recon_error values are NOT independent random noise --
3 of E2's 5 values recur in E3 to full float64 precision:
  11.390218734741211  (E2 x1, E3 x2)
  10.997451782226562  (E2 x1, E3 x1)
  11.586601257324219  (E2 x1, E3 x1)
(E2's other two values, 12.372133255004883 and 13.354048728942871, and E3's
other value, 11.782984733581543, are each unique to their own run.)

Bit-identical floats recurring across two independent 20-minute captures is a
real pattern, not coincidence. Most likely explanation (plausible, NOT verified
here): under genuinely idle/stationary conditions the underlying physical
signal (gravity + static gyro bias, both ~constant) is itself nearly constant,
and the INT8-quantized model maps near-identical inputs to a small, finite set
of possible output buckets -- one of which happens to sit just above the
deployed recon_error threshold (10.8787). This would mean the finding is a
property of (idle input) x (INT8 quantization granularity) x (this specific
threshold's proximity to a quantization bucket edge), not necessarily anything
specific to the FIFO/decimation acquisition path -- except that E1's polling
baseline (1975 windows, same nominal idle condition) saw NONE of this, which
the acquisition-method difference has not been ruled out as a contributing
factor either. Flagged as exactly this -- an open, disclosed, partially-narrowed
finding -- not resolved to a single cause. Would need a same-duration polling
rerun under identical conditions to isolate acquisition method from coincidence.
